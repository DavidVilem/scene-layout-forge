# Short request: a rigid airship

Input: "Illustrate an airship like the Hindenburg."

Interpretation: a grounded contextual illustration inspired by a rigid passenger airship, not an exact historic reconstruction or a modern inflatable blimp. Select a wide, near-broadside oblique view to preserve the long silhouette. No close-up requirement. Keep fabric skin quiet rather than invent metal rivets everywhere.

Identity anchors: research length-to-diameter ratio, tail arrangement, control-car location and engine-car arrangement before constructing geometry. For this family, roughly 245 m / 41.2 m is a useful documented scale anchor; placements in a conceptual adapter are not surveyed drawings. See [University of Stuttgart material-history account](https://www.uni-stuttgart.de/universitaet/aktuelles/meldungen/220_Tonnen_x_leichter_als_Luft._Materialgeschichte_der_Hindenburg/) and [U.S. investigation records](https://vault.fbi.gov/Hindenburg%20/Hindenburg%20Part%2003/). These sources provide facts, not required style pictures.

Secondary map: control car with framing/access; engine cars with struts and propeller clearance; mooring interface with supported platform; airship-scale hangar with framing and service access. Include people only if appropriate to the chosen activity and state their count. Do not force all far-side engines into visibility or enlarge people for readability.

Fine map: cloth seams and subtle longitudinal facets, control-car windows, engine access covers, mast joints and hangar framing. Keep some fabric regions calm. Suggested labels: CONTROL GONDOLA, ENGINE CAR, MOORING HEAD, TAIL FIN; map to visible surfaces.

Reject: squat generic envelope, oversized cabin, unsupported engine pods, material-inappropriate rivets, a hangar whose opening is visibly too small, or arrowheads landing on nearby objects. Occluded propulsion details remain unverified. Assess visual proportions after generation independently of numeric guide dimensions.

This is a worked planning case, not a tested universal airship CAD model.
