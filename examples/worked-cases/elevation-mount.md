# Worked case: orientable antenna mount

Brief: illustrate a steerable antenna with elevation drive and azimuth rotation. Pick the view that exposes the required interfaces, not a fixed screenshot.

One plausible solution: the stationary pedestal supports an azimuth bearing; the rotating fork sits on that bearing. Two trunnion bearings on the fork support the reflector cradle. A motor fixed to the fork drives a reduction unit whose output couples to a trunnion. The motor/reducer mount reacts torque into the fork. The reflector weight goes through cradle and trunnion bearings, fork, azimuth bearing and pedestal, not through an unsupported motor shaft. A right-angle reducer, sector drive or linear actuator can also be valid if its own interface and reaction path are explicit.

Reject: motor apparently touching the reflector without output connection; gearbox floating beside a shaft; trunnions on unrelated axes; a fixed brace locking the intended pivot; the rotating ring rigidly bridged to stationary railing; complete rotation inferred from a single collision-free pose. Do not infer which arrangement is real from housing size alone.

Model named motor, reducer, output, trunnions, cradle, fork, bearing and pedestal parts. Review axes and supports before rendering. If internal reduction is concealed, state that it is not verified. Make drive labels point to the housing, bearing labels to the bearing, never to the hand of the nearby inspector. A wide oblique view should expose interfaces while preserving the full assembly. After finish retrace the same paths; surface detail must not camouflage an absent coupling.

This is a reasoning case, not an executable mechanical simulation. There is no supplied image or mandatory camera, and no dismantling or animation deliverable.
