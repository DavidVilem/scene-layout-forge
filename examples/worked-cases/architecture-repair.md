# Complete facade, not accidental cutaway

Short request: "Illustrate a railway hall and make a detailed interactive maquette."

Choose a structural concept before detailing: for example, repeated long-span steel frames on outer piers, a gabled roof, full front glazing and real rectangular entrance doors. An alternative masonry vestibule is also possible, but needs its own complete volume, returns, roof and connection to the hall. Do not combine half of each just to reveal interior furniture.

Rejected candidate: a small monumental arch ends in a large glass sheet with no coherent transition to the roof. Extending that sheet fills the opening but leaves the design conflict. Record the conflict in the repair ledger, revise the primary/subordinate relationship, and generate a new design preparation. Do not preserve that small arch merely because it is in the prior pixels.

Review the new facade at whole-building scale: main span, outer supports, enclosure, entrance hierarchy and roof termination. Then inspect joints, doors and circulation. Color only the reviewed design. Build its actual arch curvature, piers, roof slopes, glazing frames and door voids in the 3D model. Use optional component opacity for glass rather than silently deleting the facade. The software comparison raster may show opaque glass; inspect transparency and interior visibility in the actual browser too.

Keep every candidate and its rejection. The source images remain complete buildings even though the HTML offers optional cuts. A different brief may call for a flat-roof entrance, masonry arches, an open pavilion or a section; this example is not a universal station template.
