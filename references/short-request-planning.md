# Develop a short request into a credible scene

Use this before geometry for underspecified technical illustrations. The user supplies intent, not a full bill of materials. Expand it into a concise scene contract without requiring a longer prompt, private pictures or earlier conversation. Explicit omissions, counts and projection always prevail.

Start history automatically and resolve annotations from the request, not example defaults; see [execution policy](execution-policy.md). A short prompt still requires a checked guide.

## Intent and identity before surface detail

Distinguish an immersive scene, an explanatory study and a deliberately isolated object. A local cutaway can coexist with an immersive environment; it does not require simplifying the whole scene. Choose a view for the subject, with no mandatory foreground, frontal symmetry or standard corridor.

For a named real subject, consult suitable primary documentation for a small set of identity anchors: silhouette/proportions, characteristic component arrangement, material behavior and operating setting. Record sources and distinguish documented facts from conceptual placement. For "like X", preserve defining morphology while declaring interpretation; do not claim an exact replica. Use textual facts without needing reference images. If research is unavailable, disclose uncertainty rather than fabricate specifications. A generic request must not silently become a specific historic model.

## Design three scales

Before detailing, explore several spatial organizations using [design exploration](design-exploration.md). Keep the seed, chosen program and actual realization mapping. Choose alternate massing, circulation and adjacency where the brief permits; changing surface texture or camera alone does not satisfy variety. Examples supply component construction patterns, never the default floorplan. User-specified repetition or a fixed seed takes precedence.

Write a compact table of visible regions before building:

| Scale | Decision | Evidence in the guide or finish |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Envelope, main architecture, circulation, proportions | Correct silhouette and readable spatial organization |
| Secondary | Functional assemblies, interfaces and context | Attached frames, access structures, service routes or domain-appropriate subassemblies; not just rows of fasteners |
| Fine | Construction and material cues | Seams, closures, brackets and joints at meaningful locations |

Include secondary systems where they explain use or construction, even when omitted from a short prompt. Do not invent hidden machinery, unsafe activity or precision engineering claims. Smooth fabric envelopes and open circulation may remain quiet; do not add pipes, rivets or fake panel density to compensate. Allocate detail by visible function and available pixels, not equal coverage everywhere.

Plan subject, immediate setting and depth together. Use overlaps and continuation outside the frame where suitable, while keeping essential connections and labels visible. Supporting architecture can be richly developed without competing through equal contrast. Context is not a generic warehouse pasted behind every object. Human activity is optional; when strongly implied by the task, choose a small purposeful cast and declare the assumption. Never override exact counts or a request for no people.

## Guide and finish responsibilities

Check identity, secondary assemblies and spatial layers before rendering. Do not approve a sparse guide solely because its counts pass. Major geometry belongs in the guide; declare small planned details in the handoff. Missing essential shapes require an explicit adapter, not unrelated fixture geometry. Describe what the guide validates and what it only approximates.

Retain expressive animation-layout mark-making: selective strong contour accents, medium structural lines, fine joints, quiet highlights and localized graphite shadows. Mechanical credibility does not require sterile CAD linework; hand-drawn character does not justify warped axes. Never use repeated hatch texture as a substitute for assemblies.

Review independently: (1) subject identity and proportions, (2) contacts and functional relationships, (3) secondary construction and depth, (4) drawing style and tone, (5) exact annotation targets. A failure in one is not cured by passing another. Follow the existing correction limit. Report occluded/unverified features.

Use [coherence validation](coherence-validation.md) on the complete physical model. Openings must remove solid host geometry, furniture must reflect its actual use, and circulation volumes must reach entries without traversing walls. A section is derived only afterward. Do not satisfy a six-level request by rendering six copies of one undifferentiated shelf or six elevator-shaped cabinets.

For very large subjects, compare human silhouettes and nearby service objects against a known dimension at similar depth. The finisher can enlarge people to make them legible even when the guide is correct. Prefer correctly small figures over artificial legibility, and report residual scale mismatch after bounded corrections.

## Behavioral checks

Use the same short request in a fresh session without pictures. Keep tool, output size and intended view comparable when diagnosing differences. Inspect the actual expanded contract and guide as well as final pixels; equal user prompts do not establish equal image-generator inputs. Do not claim visual improvement from textual checks alone.
