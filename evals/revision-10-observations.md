# Revision 2026-11-13.10

The new dry-dock example registers its hull and secondary assemblies, samples
six keel contact patches, declares inlet/outlet boundaries and a shared discharge
header, and freezes the scene before finishing. Custom geometry mutations after
validation and semantic route changes during correction are rejected.

Two completed artistic trials used only current calculated guides and written
direction. The first brief requested a dry dock, pump station, five workers and
English annotations. The second was only "Dique seco." The second retained five
workers and a readable intake/discharge separation, but excessive sky/floor and
surface hatching did not meet the intended construction-detail richness. It is
not accepted as evidence of visual parity with a dense architectural layout.

Camera review exposed clipped access and figure occlusion in intermediate guides;
both were revised before finishing. A subsequent code inspection found that
horizontal cable-tray orientation was ignored by the old adapter. The adapter
now rotates the crossbars and cross section, with failure tests. The dock now
uses flat-top wall corbels and a grating walkway.

The third trial used "Dique seco con cinco trabajadores." and a closer oblique
guide. Five workers, two pumps, six keel blocks and five callouts were visible;
tray detail and component readability improved. However, the finish drew a blind
cap on the external discharge boundary and underlined labels; the setting became
too isolated. This trial is not accepted. The correction contract now explicitly
distinguishes external continuation from sealed termination. Boundary geometry
and registered contextual development were revised in a fourth design iteration.

The fourth trial used only "Dique seco." and a new registered guide with quay,
workshop, open external bores and oriented services. One ship, two pumps, five
people and five readable English annotations were observed. Fine seams, grating,
brackets, flange bolts and cabinet details remained legible. A correction moved
the pump arrow from motor to casing. Visible pump/header routes retained their
destinations across both corrections. The generated image nevertheless retained
seven keel blocks instead of the contracted six. The two-correction limit was
respected: this is an unresolved count failure, not a passed contact evaluation.
Underground intakes and the external outfall are unmodeled; only visible segments
were reviewed. The result is a detailed conceptual illustration, not a verified
operating dock or proof of universal quality across clients.

Four design trials used short/very short briefs, calculated guides and written
style only. No guide artwork from the conversation was supplied to the generator.
Their failures informed geometry registration, projection review, the explicit
component identity map and correction semantics rather than only more adjectives.

Numerical checks and image acceptance are separate. Surface-contact samples do
not prove hull stability, loads, process performance or hidden raster routes.
No artwork, private run paths or user references belong in this package.
